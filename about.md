I write across form—novels, poetry, essays, and screenplays—drawn to questions of home, belief, systems, and the natural world.

My work is shaped by a Southern upbringing, early roots in Mormon religious culture, and a long, complicated relationship with academia. I am equally drawn to scientific ways of knowing and to mythic or narrative ones, and much of my writing lives at that intersection.

I care deeply about landscape—Appalachian woods, high desert air, mountain light—and about how place shapes memory, language, and belonging. Fiction and fantasy, especially, offer ways to explore those tensions without flattening them.

I prefer a degree of anonymity. The work, ideally, should speak first. This site functions as both archive and workshop: some pieces are finished, others ongoing.
